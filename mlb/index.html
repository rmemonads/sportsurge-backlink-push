What Is MLB?

Major League Baseball, commonly abbreviated as MLB, is the highest level of professional baseball in the United States and Canada. Founded in 1903, MLB is composed of 30 teams divided equally between the American League (AL) and the National League (NL). The league is widely regarded as the oldest of the four major professional sports leagues in North America and remains one of the most popular sports organizations in the world.

MLB governs the Regular Season, All-Star Game, and Postseason, including the World Series, which has been the championship series of professional baseball since 1903. The league currently operates under a collective bargaining agreement between the MLB Commissioner's Office and the MLB Players Association (MLBPA). Rob Manfred serves as the current Commissioner of Baseball, having assumed the role in January 2015.

The MLB season is the longest of any major professional sport, with each team playing 162 regular-season games between late March and late September. This marathon schedule means there is nearly always a game available to watch every single day during the season, making an MLB live stream one of the most in-demand streaming options for sports fans worldwide.

MLB has a rich history that spans over a century. The league has produced some of the greatest athletes in sports history, including Babe Ruth, Ted Williams, Hank Aaron, Willie Mays, Jackie Robinson, Sandy Koufax, Nolan Ryan, Ken Griffey Jr., Derek Jeter, and many more. The sport's traditions, rivalries, and postseason drama continue to captivate millions of fans across the globe.

Over the years, MLB has embraced modern technology and broadcasting innovations. From the early days of radio broadcasts to the current era of digital streaming, the league has adapted to ensure fans can watch games however they prefer. Today, games are broadcast on networks including ESPN, Fox, FS1, TBS, MLB Network, Apple TV+, Peacock, and Amazon Prime Video. MLB Teams

Major League Baseball is made up of 30 teams organized into two leagues and six divisions. The American League consists of 15 teams split into the East, Central, and West divisions. The National League also consists of 15 teams split into the East, Central, and West divisions. Below is a complete list of all 30 MLB teams.

American League East

The American League East is one of the most competitive divisions in baseball, featuring several of the sport's most storied franchises. The New York Yankees are the most successful franchise in MLB history with 27 World Series championships. Playing at Yankee Stadium in the Bronx, New York, the Yankees have been home to legends like Babe Ruth, Lou Gehrig, Joe DiMaggio, Mickey Mantle, and Derek Jeter. The Boston Red Sox play at Fenway Park, the oldest active ballpark in MLB, built in 1912. The Red Sox broke the famous Curse of the Bambino by winning the 2004 World Series and have added three more titles since. The Tampa Bay Rays play at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, Florida, and have emerged as a competitive franchise known for their analytical approach to the game. The Toronto Blue Jays are the only Canadian team in MLB, playing at Rogers Centre in Toronto. They won back-to-back World Series titles in 1992 and 1993. The Baltimore Orioles play at Oriole Park at Camden Yards, a ballpark credited with revolutionizing modern stadium design when it opened in 1992.

American League Central

The AL Central features teams from the Midwest. The Cleveland Guardians play at Progressive Field in Cleveland, Ohio, and have been competitive in recent years with a strong pitching rotation. The Minnesota Twins play at Target Field in Minneapolis and have won three World Series titles in franchise history, including two consecutive in 1987 and 1991. The Detroit Tigers play at Comerica Park and boast a rich baseball tradition with four World Series championships. The Chicago White Sox play at Guaranteed Rate Field on the South Side of Chicago. They won the 2005 World Series, their first title in 88 years. The Kansas City Royals play at Kauffman Stadium and captured back-to-back pennants in 2014 and 2015, winning the World Series in 2015.

American League West

The AL West includes teams from the western United States. The Houston Astros play at Minute Maid Park and have become one of the dominant teams of the 2010s and 2020s, winning the World Series in 2017 and 2022. The Texas Rangers play at Globe Life Field in Arlington, Texas, and won their first World Series championship in 2023. The Seattle Mariners play at T-Mobile Park and are known for their passionate fanbase and the legacy of Ken Griffey Jr., who hit 417 home runs for the franchise. The Los Angeles Angels play at Angel Stadium of Anaheim and have been home to generational talents like Mike Trout and Shohei Ohtani. The Oakland Athletics play at the Oakland Coliseum and have won nine World Series titles throughout their storied history, including three consecutive from 1972 to 1974.

National League East

The NL East features some of baseball's most passionate fanbases. The Atlanta Braves play at Truist Park in Cobb County, Georgia, and won the 2021 World Series. The Braves have a storied history that includes 18 National League pennants. The Philadelphia Phillies play at Citizens Bank Park and are the oldest professional sports franchise in the United States, established in 1883. They won the World Series in 2008 and returned to the Fall Classic in 2022 and 2023. The New York Mets play at Citi Field in Flushing, Queens. The Mets won the World Series in 1969 and 1986 and remain a major draw in the New York market. The Miami Marlins play at LoanDepot Park and have won two World Series titles (1997 and 2003) in their relatively short history. The Washington Nationals play at Nationals Park and won the 2019 World Series in dramatic fashion, coming back from a 3-1 series deficit against the Houston Astros.

National League Central

The NL Central is known for its fierce rivalries. The Milwaukee Brewers play at American Family Field in Milwaukee and have been consistent contenders in the National League. The Chicago Cubs play at Wrigley Field, the second-oldest ballpark in MLB, built in 1914. The Cubs broke a 108-year championship drought by winning the 2016 World Series in one of the most memorable fall classics ever played. The Cincinnati Reds play at Great American Ball Park and are recognized as the oldest professional baseball franchise, dating back to 1869. The St. Louis Cardinals play at Busch Stadium and are the most successful franchise in the National League with 11 World Series championships. The Pittsburgh Pirates play at PNC Park, widely considered one of the best ballparks in America, and have won five World Series titles in franchise history.

National League West

The NL West features teams from California, Arizona, and Colorado. The Los Angeles Dodgers play at Dodger Stadium, the largest MLB stadium by seating capacity with 56,000 seats. The Dodgers are one of baseball's most successful franchises with seven World Series championships and have been perennial contenders throughout the 2020s. The San Diego Padres play at Petco Park in downtown San Diego and have built a competitive roster with stars like Manny Machado and Fernando Tatis Jr. The San Francisco Giants play at Oracle Park and won three World Series titles in five years (2010, 2012, 2014), one of the most impressive runs in modern baseball. The Arizona Diamondbacks play at Chase Field and won the 2001 World Series just four years after the franchise was established. The Colorado Rockies play at Coors Field in Denver, located at an elevation of 5,200 feet, which creates unique hitting conditions due to the thin mountain air.

MLB Live Schedule

The MLB season runs from late March through October, making it the longest season in professional sports. With 30 teams each playing 162 regular-season games, that totals 2,430 games over approximately six months. Add in the postseason, and there are well over 2,500 games per season.

MLB games are scheduled throughout the day, with start times varying by timezone and broadcast window. Weekday games typically start in the early evening, with first pitches at 1:05 PM, 4:05 PM, 6:05 PM, 7:05 PM, or 7:10 PM local time. Weekend afternoon games often feature 1:05 PM or 1:35 PM start times, while marquee national broadcasts on ESPN, Fox, or Apple TV+ may have prime-time slots at 7:00 PM or 8:00 PM Eastern.

During the regular season, every team plays games nearly every day. Monday through Thursday features a full slate of interleague and intra-division matchups. Friday through Sunday brings weekend series with often three or four games between the same two teams. National holidays like Memorial Day, Independence Day, and Labor Day feature special MLB event games with expanded coverage.

The All-Star break typically occurs in mid-July, providing a brief pause in the regular season. The MLB All-Star Game, also known as the Midsummer Classic, features the best players from the American League facing off against the National League. The Home Run Derby, held the night before the All-Star Game, is one of the most exciting events in all of sports.

September is an especially busy month as teams make their final push for playoff positioning. Rosters expand from 26 to 28 players on September 1, giving teams additional depth for the final month. The last day of the regular season, typically the first Sunday in October, often features dramatic finishes as teams battle for division titles and wild card spots.

After the regular season concludes, the MLB Postseason begins with the Wild Card Series, followed by the Division Series, Championship Series, and culminating with the World Series in late October or early November. MLB Season Format

The MLB season is structured in several distinct phases, each with its own significance and excitement. Understanding the season format helps fans appreciate the journey from Opening Day to the World Series.

Spring Training

Spring Training begins in mid-February and runs through late March. Teams split into two groups for exhibition games: the Grapefruit League in Florida and the Cactus League in Arizona. Spring Training gives fans their first look at the year's rosters and provides an opportunity for young prospects to compete for roster spots. Teams play approximately 30-35 exhibition games during this period. Notable Spring Training venues include George M. Steinbrenner Field in Tampa for the New York Yankees, Camelback Ranch in Phoenix for the Los Angeles Dodgers, and City of Palms Park in Fort Myers for the Boston Red Sox.

Regular Season

The Regular Season begins on a Thursday in late March, known as Opening Day. Each of the 30 teams plays 162 games: 81 home games and 81 away games. Teams play 19 games against each of their four division rivals, totaling 76 division games. The remaining 86 games are played against non-division opponents, with a balanced interleague schedule. The Regular Season concludes in late September or early October. The schedule is designed so that every team plays every day, with off-days scattered throughout the season for travel and rest.

MLB has implemented several rule changes in recent years to improve pace of play and fan experience. The pitch clock, introduced in 2023, limits pitchers to 15 seconds with bases empty and 20 seconds with runners on base. The larger bases, increased from 15 inches to 18 inches square, encourage more stolen base attempts. The defensive shift restrictions require two infielders to be on each side of second base, leading to more hits and action on the field.

Division Structure

MLB's 30 teams are divided into six divisions. The American League East includes the New York Yankees, Boston Red Sox, Tampa Bay Rays, Toronto Blue Jays, and Baltimore Orioles. The American League Central includes the Cleveland Guardians, Minnesota Twins, Detroit Tigers, Chicago White Sox, and Kansas City Royals. The American League West includes the Houston Astros, Texas Rangers, Seattle Mariners, Los Angeles Angels, and Oakland Athletics. The National League East includes the Atlanta Braves, Philadelphia Phillies, New York Mets, Miami Marlins, and Washington Nationals. The National League Central includes the Milwaukee Brewers, Chicago Cubs, Cincinnati Reds, St. Louis Cardinals, and Pittsburgh Pirates. The National League West includes the Los Angeles Dodgers, San Diego Padres, San Francisco Giants, Arizona Diamondbacks, and Colorado Rockies.

Interleague Play

Since 2023, MLB has adopted a balanced schedule where every team plays every other team at least once per season. Interleague games now account for 16 of a team's 162 games. This change was made to increase competitive balance and give fans the chance to see matchups between teams from different leagues more frequently. Historic interleague rivalries like the Subway Series between the New York Yankees and New York Mets, the Freeway Series between the Los Angeles Dodgers and Los Angeles Angels, and the Crosstown Classic between the Chicago Cubs and Chicago White Sox are highlighted each season.

MLB Playoffs

The MLB Postseason is one of the most thrilling events in professional sports. The format has evolved over the years, and the current system features 12 teams competing in a single-elimination and best-of-seven format to determine the World Series champion.

How Teams Qualify

Six teams from each league qualify for the postseason. The three division winners in each league (East, Central, West) receive automatic bids and are seeded 1 through 3 based on regular-season record. The three teams with the best records among non-division winners earn wild card spots and are seeded 4 through 6. Tiebreaker scenarios are resolved using regular-season head-to-head records, division records, and ultimately a tiebreaker game if needed.

Wild Card Series

The postseason begins with the Wild Card Series, a best-of-three format. The 3rd seed hosts the 6th seed, and the 4th seed hosts the 5th seed. The 1st and 2nd seeds receive a first-round bye and advance directly to the Division Series. The Wild Card Series takes place in the first few days of October, typically over a long weekend. These games create an electric atmosphere as teams fight to advance.

Division Series (ALDS and NLDS)

The Division Series is a best-of-seven format. In the American League Division Series (ALDS), the top-seeded team faces the lowest remaining seed, while the second-seeded team faces the other remaining seed. The same structure applies to the National League Division Series (NLDS). The Division Series typically takes place over 7-10 days in mid-October. Home-field advantage goes to the higher-seeded team in a 2-2-1 format.

Championship Series (ALCS and NLCS)

The League Championship Series determines which team from each league advances to the World Series. Both the American League Championship Series (ALCS) and National League Championship Series (NLCS) are best-of-seven formats. These series are played in a 2-3-2 format with home-field advantage going to the team with the better regular-season record. The ALCS and NLCS are among the most-watched baseball broadcasts of the year, often featuring dramatic moments and memorable performances.

The World Series

The World Series is the championship series of Major League Baseball and the culmination of the MLB postseason. Played annually since 1903, it is a best-of-seven matchup between the American League champion and the National League champion. The World Series is often referred to as the Fall Classic and is considered one of the most prestigious events in all of professional sports.

The series is played in a 2-2-1-1-1 format, with home-field advantage going to the team with the better regular-season record. Games are played in late October or early November, depending on the length of the preceding postseason series. The World Series typically generates enormous television ratings and is broadcast on Fox in the United States.

The New York Yankees hold the record for the most World Series championships with 27 titles. The St. Louis Cardinals are second with 11 championships, followed by the Oakland Athletics with 9, the Boston Red Sox with 9, and the San Francisco Giants with 8. The Los Angeles Dodgers, with 7 championships, have been the most dominant team of the 2020s era.

Some of the most memorable World Series moments in history include the New York Mets' miraculous comeback in 1986, the Arizona Diamondbacks' walk-off victory in 2001 against the Yankees, the Boston Red Sox reversing the Curse of the Bambino in 2004, the Chicago Cubs breaking a 108-year drought in 2016, and the Washington Nationals' stunning road victory in 2019. Each World Series brings new drama and creates lasting memories for baseball fans around the world.

The World Series MVP award, officially known as the Willie Mays World Series MVP Award, is given to the most outstanding player in the championship series. Notable recipients include Babe Ruth, Mickey Mantle, Reggie Jackson, Madison Bumgarner, and many others who delivered legendary performances on baseball's biggest stage.
