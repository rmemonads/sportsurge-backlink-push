FIFA World Cup 2026

The FIFA World Cup 2026 will be the 23rd edition of the FIFA World Cup and the first to feature 48 teams, expanded from the previous 32-team format. The tournament will be jointly hosted by the United States, Canada, and Mexico, making it the first World Cup to be hosted by three nations and the first to be held across two confederations (UEFA and CONCACAF).

Host Cities and Stadiums

The tournament will be played across 16 cities and 16 stadiums in North America:

United States (11 cities): MetLife Stadium (East Rutherford, New Jersey), AT&T Stadium (Arlington, Texas), Arrowhead Stadium (Kansas City, Missouri), Mercedes-Benz Stadium (Atlanta, Georgia), NRG Stadium (Houston, Texas), Lumen Field (Seattle, Washington), Levi's Stadium (Santa Clara, California), Hard Rock Stadium (Miami Gardens, Florida), Lincoln Financial Field (Philadelphia, Pennsylvania), Gillette Stadium (Foxborough, Massachusetts), and BMO Field (Toronto, Canada – shared).

Mexico (3 cities): Estadio Azteca (Mexico City), Estadio BBVA (Monterrey), and Estadio Akron (Guadalajara).

Canada (2 cities): BMO Field (Toronto) and BC Place (Vancouver).

The Estadio Azteca in Mexico City will make history by hosting World Cup matches for the third time, having previously hosted the 1970 and 1986 finals. The tournament final will be played at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey.

Tournament Format

The expanded 48-team format will feature 12 groups of four teams each. The top two teams from each group plus the eight best third-placed teams will advance to a 32-team knockout round. This means 104 total matches will be played over the course of the tournament, up from 64 in the 32-team format. The tournament will begin on June 11, 2026, and the final will be played on July 19, 2026.

UEFA Champions League

The UEFA Champions League is the most prestigious club competition in world football. It brings together the best teams from across Europe to compete for the trophy that every club dreams of lifting. The tournament's anthem, one of the most recognizable pieces of music in sports, signals the start of some of football's most dramatic nights.

History and Legacy

The Champions League began as the European Cup in 1955 and was rebranded in 1992. Real Madrid hold the record for most titles with 15, including a record-breaking run of four consecutive titles between 2016 and 2019. AC Milan (7), Bayern Munich (6), Barcelona (5), Liverpool (6), and Manchester United (3) are among the other multiple winners. The competition has produced some of the most iconic moments in football history, from Sergio Agüero's last-minute title winner for Manchester City to Liverpool's miraculous comeback against Barcelona in the 2019 semi-final.

Current Format

The Champions League has undergone a significant format change starting from the 2024-25 season. The traditional group stage has been replaced with a 36-team league phase where each team plays eight matches against eight different opponents. The top eight teams advance directly to the Round of 16, while teams ranked 9th through 24th play a knockout playoff round. Teams ranked 25th through 36th are eliminated from European competition entirely.

This new format guarantees more high-quality matches between elite clubs during the league phase, as traditional "group of death" scenarios are replaced with a more balanced distribution of opponents.
