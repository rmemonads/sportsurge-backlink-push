What Is the NHL?

The National Hockey League (NHL) is the premier professional ice hockey league in North America. Founded in 1917, the NHL consists of 32 teams – 25 in the United States and 7 in Canada. The league is widely regarded as the top level of professional hockey in the world and attracts millions of viewers every season through its fast-paced, physical style of play.

The NHL regular season typically runs from October through April, with each team playing 82 games. Following the regular season, 16 teams qualify for the Stanley Cup Playoffs, a grueling four-round elimination tournament that culminates in the Stanley Cup Finals. The Stanley Cup itself is the oldest professional sports trophy in North America, first awarded in 1893.

The league is divided into two conferences: the Eastern Conference and the Western Conference. Each conference is further split into two divisions of eight teams each. The Eastern Conference comprises the Atlantic Division and the Metropolitan Division, while the Western Conference consists of the Central Division and the Pacific Division. This structure ensures geographic rivalries remain intense and scheduling is efficient across North American time zones.

Hockey is a sport of speed, skill, and physicality. Players skate at speeds exceeding 30 miles per hour, shoot pucks at over 100 miles per hour, and engage in body checks that make the NHL one of the most exciting spectator sports. The NHL also hosts the annual NHL All-Star Game, the NHL Entry Draft, and special outdoor events like the Winter Classic and the Stadium Series, which draw massive crowds in iconic venues.

The league's television and digital rights are held by major broadcasters including ESPN, TNT, ABC, Sportsnet, and TVA Sports in Canada. In the United States, ESPN and TNT carry the bulk of regular season and playoff games, while ABC broadcasts select marquee matchups and the Stanley Cup Finals. Sportsnet and TSN provide comprehensive coverage for Canadian viewers. These broadcast deals have made the NHL accessible to fans across the globe. NHL Season Format

The NHL season is structured into three main phases: the preseason, the regular season, and the playoffs. Understanding this format helps fans follow the action from start to finish.

Preseason

The NHL preseason takes place in September and early October. Each team plays approximately six to eight exhibition games. Preseason games give coaches an opportunity to evaluate prospects, test new line combinations, and prepare for the regular season. While preseason games do not count in the standings, they generate excitement among fans eager for hockey to return. Rookie tournaments are also held during this period, featuring young players competing for roster spots.

Regular Season

The NHL regular season consists of 82 games per team, beginning in early October and concluding in mid-April. Each team plays 41 home games and 41 away games. The schedule is designed so that teams face every other team in the league at least twice, with additional games against divisional rivals. Under the current schedule format, teams play their division rivals four times per season and play teams from the other conference twice. This balanced schedule ensures that every team has an equal opportunity to qualify for the playoffs.

The regular season includes several marquee events that draw massive viewership. The NHL Winter Classic, held outdoors on New Year's Day, features two teams playing in a unique outdoor setting at a major stadium or arena. The NHL Stadium Series brings outdoor games to different cities throughout the season. The NHL All-Star Skills Competition and All-Star Game showcase the league's top talent in a fun, fan-friendly format. These events are all available as NHL live streams on Sportsurge.

Points in the NHL are awarded as follows: a win in regulation time earns 2 points, a win in overtime or a shootout earns 2 points, an overtime or shootout loss earns 1 point, and a regulation loss earns 0 points. This point system rewards teams for winning while also providing a consolation point for teams that push games to overtime. At the end of the regular season, the top three teams in each division qualify for the playoffs, along with two wild card teams from each conference, for a total of 16 playoff teams.

All-Star Break

The NHL All-Star break typically occurs in late January or early February. During this period, the regular season is paused and the league hosts its All-Star festivities. The All-Star Game features a tournament format with teams representing each division. The Skills Competition includes events like the Fastest Skater, Hardest Shot, Accuracy Shooting, and the Breakaway Challenge. These events highlight the incredible athleticism and skill of NHL players and are among the most-watched non-game events in professional sports.

Trade Deadline

The NHL Trade Deadline falls in early March and is one of the most exciting days on the NHL calendar. Teams rush to make trades to strengthen their rosters for the playoff push. Contending teams acquire veteran players and rental assets, while rebuilding teams trade away expiring contracts for draft picks and prospects. The trade deadline generates enormous buzz and Sportsurge provides comprehensive coverage and analysis of all deadline-day transactions.

NHL Playoffs

The NHL Playoffs are one of the most intense and exciting postseason tournaments in professional sports. Sixteen teams qualify for the playoffs – eight from the Eastern Conference and eight from the Western Conference. The playoffs begin in mid-April and can extend into late June, with the Stanley Cup Finals determining the league champion.

Playoff Format

The NHL playoff format follows a bracket structure. In each conference, the top three teams in each division are seeded 1 through 3 based on their regular season points. Two wild card teams are then added, with the wild card team with the most points facing the division winner with the fewest points. The other wild card team faces the division winner with the most points. The second and third place teams within each division face each other in the first round.

Each playoff series is a best-of-seven format, meaning the first team to win four games advances to the next round. The playoffs consist of four rounds: the First Round, the Second Round (also known as the Division Finals), the Conference Finals, and the Stanley Cup Finals. The intensity ramps up with each round as teams battle through grueling series that often go the full seven games.

First Round

The First Round features eight series across both conferences. Upsets are common in the opening round, as lower-seeded teams often catch higher-seeded opponents off guard. The speed and physicality of playoff hockey is unlike anything seen during the regular season. Every hit, every blocked shot, and every save matters in the First Round. Second Round

The Second Round narrows the field to four teams per conference. By this point, the remaining teams have proven they can win in the playoffs and the competition becomes even fiercer. Rivalries intensify and the physical toll of playoff hockey becomes evident. Teams that succeed in the Second Round are typically those with deep rosters, strong goaltending, and veteran leadership. Games in this round often feature dramatic overtime finishes that keep fans on the edge of their seats.

Conference Finals

The Conference Finals determine which team from each conference advances to the Stanley Cup Finals. These series are among the most-watched NHL games of the year. The Eastern Conference Finals and Western Conference Finals feature the best remaining teams battling for the right to compete for the Stanley Cup. The atmosphere in the arenas during Conference Final games is electric, with fans creating a deafening environment that can swing the momentum of a series.

Playoff Overtime

One of the unique aspects of the NHL playoffs is the overtime format. Unlike the regular season, playoff overtime is played at full strength (5-on-5) with no shootout. Overtime periods are 20 minutes each and continue until someone scores a goal. This sudden-death format creates some of the most dramatic moments in sports history. The Stanley Cup

The Stanley Cup is the championship trophy of the National Hockey League and is widely considered one of the most prestigious trophies in all of professional sports. Originally donated by Sir Frederick Arthur Stanley, the Governor General of Canada, in 1893, the Stanley Cup has been awarded every year except for 1919 (due to the Spanish flu pandemic) and 2005 (due to the NHL lockout).

What makes the Stanley Cup unique among major professional sports trophies is that it is not recreated each year. Instead, the same trophy has been passed from champion to champion for over a century. The names of every player, coach, and staff member on the winning team are engraved on the Cup's bands. When the bands become full, the oldest band is removed and retired to the Hockey Hall of Fame in Toronto, making room for a new band.

The tradition of allowing each player on the winning team to spend a day with the Stanley Cup is one of the most beloved traditions in sports. Players take the Cup to their hometowns, share it with family and friends, and create lasting memories. Some of the most famous Stanley Cup moments include players drinking champagne from the Cup, skating with it on frozen ponds, and bringing it to hospitals and community events.

The current Stanley Cup has a height of 35.25 inches and weighs 34.5 pounds. It consists of a bowl atop a barrel with five bands of silver, each capable of holding 13 teams' names. The bowl was added in 1963 when the original bowl became too small. The Cup is cared for by a dedicated keeper, Phil Pritchard, who travels with it to every event and ceremony.

Winning the Stanley Cup requires a team to win 16 playoff games across four rounds. This makes it one of the most difficult championships to win in professional sports. The physical and mental toll of the playoffs is immense, with players battling through injuries, fatigue, and the pressure of elimination games. The team that ultimately lifts the Cup has proven itself to be the most resilient and talented group in the league.

Some of the most successful franchises in Stanley Cup history include the Montreal Canadiens with 24 titles, the Toronto Maple Leafs with 13, the Detroit Red Wings with 11, the Boston Bruins with 6, and the Chicago Blackhawks with 6. In the modern era, the Pittsburgh Penguins, Tampa Bay Lightning, and Colorado Avalanche have established themselves as perennial contenders.
