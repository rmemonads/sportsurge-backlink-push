What Is Cricket?

Cricket is a bat-and-ball sport played between two teams of eleven players on a field. It is one of the most popular sports in the world, with over 2.5 billion fans globally. The sport is especially popular in India, Australia, England, Pakistan, South Africa, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, the West Indies, and New Zealand. Beyond cricket, fans can also stream soccer, NFL, and boxing live on Sportsurge.

Cricket is governed internationally by the International Cricket Council (ICC), which organizes major tournaments including the Cricket World Cup, T20 World Cup, and Champions Trophy. The sport has three main formats: Test cricket, One Day Internationals (ODIs), and Twenty20 (T20), each offering a different style of play.

Test cricket is the oldest and longest format, played over five days with unlimited overs. ODIs feature 50 overs per side, while T20 cricket, the shortest format, features 20 overs per side and is known for its fast-paced, explosive gameplay. The rise of T20 leagues like the Indian Premier League (IPL) has brought cricket to new global audiences.

Cricket matches are played on oval or circular fields with a 22-yard pitch in the center. The batting team tries to score runs by hitting the ball bowled by the opposing team, while the bowling team tries to dismiss batsmen and limit runs. The team with the most runs at the end wins.

Major cricket stadiums around the world include Lord's Cricket Ground in London (known as the "Home of Cricket"), the Melbourne Cricket Ground (MCG) in Australia, Eden Gardens in Kolkata, India, The Oval in London, and the Sydney Cricket Ground (SCG). These iconic venues host some of the most anticipated matches in the sport.

Cricket Formats

Test Cricket

Test cricket is the longest and oldest format of the game, played over five days with two innings per team. Each day features up to 90 overs of play. Test matches are played between national teams, and the format is considered the ultimate test of a player's skill, endurance, and mental toughness. The ICC organizes the World Test Championship (WTC), a league culminating in a final between the top two ranked teams.

One Day Internationals (ODIs)

ODIs are limited-overs cricket matches where each team bats for 50 overs. The format was introduced in the 1970s and has since become one of the most popular cricket formats. The Cricket World Cup, held every four years, is the premier ODI tournament. Notable ODI events include the 1983 World Cup (India's first victory), the 1999 World Cup (Australia's dominance), and the 2019 World Cup (England's dramatic final against New Zealand).

T20 Cricket

T20 is the shortest international format, with each team batting for 20 overs. T20 cricket is known for its explosive batting, innovative shots, and fast-paced action. The ICC T20 World Cup is held every two years. Domestic T20 leagues like the Indian Premier League (IPL), Big Bash League (BBL), Caribbean Premier League (CPL), and The Hundred have made T20 cricket the most watched format globally.

The Hundred

The Hundred is a professional cricket tournament launched by the England and Wales Cricket Board (ECB) in 2021. Instead of overs, each team faces 100 balls. The tournament features eight city-based teams and has introduced cricket to new audiences in the UK.

Major Cricket Tournaments

ICC Cricket World Cup

The ICC Cricket World Cup is the premier international cricket tournament, held every four years since 1975. The tournament features the top 10-14 national teams competing in ODI format. Australia holds the record with five World Cup titles (1987, 1999, 2003, 2007, 2023). India has won twice (1983, 2011), while West Indies (1975, 1979), Pakistan (1992), Sri Lanka (1996), and England (2019) have each won once. The 2027 Cricket World Cup will be co-hosted by South Africa, Zimbabwe, and Namibia.

ICC T20 World Cup

The ICC T20 World Cup is the premier international T20 cricket tournament, held every two years since 2007. West Indies won the inaugural edition in 2007. Other winners include India (2007), England (2010, 2022), Sri Lanka (2014), West Indies again (2012, 2016), Australia (2021), and England (2022). The tournament features 16-20 teams and is one of the most-watched cricket events globally.

Indian Premier League (IPL)

The IPL is the world's most popular domestic T20 cricket league, featuring franchise teams from Indian cities. Founded in 2008, the IPL features star players from around the world. The league consists of 10 teams: Mumbai Indians (MI), Chennai Super Kings (CSK), Royal Challengers Bangalore (RCB), Kolkata Knight Riders (KKR), Delhi Capitals (DC), Sunrisers Hyderabad (SRH), Rajasthan Royals (RR), Punjab Kings (PBKS), Lucknow Super Giants (LSG), and Gujarat Titans (GT). The IPL season runs from March to May each year.

The Ashes

The Ashes is a historic Test cricket series played between England and Australia since 1882. The series is named after a satirical obituary published in 1882 stating that English cricket had died and "the body will be cremated and the ashes taken to Australia." The series alternates between England and Australia, with each team hosting five Tests. Australia currently holds the Ashes. Legendary Ashes moments include Don Bradman's 1930 tour, Botham's Ashes in 1981, and Ben Stokes' Headingley heroics in 2019.

Big Bash League (BBL)

The Big Bash League is Australia's premier domestic T20 cricket league, featuring eight city-based teams. Teams include Sydney Sixers, Sydney Thunder, Melbourne Stars, Melbourne Renegades, Perth Scorchers, Brisbane Heat, Adelaide Strikers, and Hobart Hurricanes. The BBL season runs from December to February and attracts top international players.

Caribbean Premier League (CPL)

The CPL is the Caribbean's premier T20 cricket league featuring six franchise teams. Teams include Trinbago Knight Riders, Guyana Amazon Warriors, Barbados Royals, St Lucia Kings, St Kitts and Nevis Patriots, and Antigua and Barbuda Falcons. The CPL is known for its vibrant atmosphere, music, and entertainment alongside competitive cricket.

ICC Champions Trophy

The ICC Champions Trophy is a premier international ODI tournament held every four years. Often called the "Mini World Cup," it features the top eight ODI teams. Previous winners include Australia, India, South Africa, West Indies, New Zealand, and Sri Lanka.

Cricket Teams

India National Cricket Team

India is one of cricket's most successful and popular teams. With over 1.4 billion fans, Indian cricket generates massive viewership. India has won the Cricket World Cup twice (1983, 2011), the T20 World Cup once (2007), and the Champions Trophy twice (2002, 2013). The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) is the wealthiest cricket board in the world. Key players include Virat Kohli, Rohit Sharma, Jasprit Bumrah, and Shubman Gill. India's home venues include Eden Gardens (Kolkata), Wankhede Stadium (Mumbai), and Narendra Modi Stadium (Ahmedabad), the world's largest cricket stadium.

Australia National Cricket Team

Australia is the most successful cricket nation, with five Cricket World Cup titles (1987, 1999, 2003, 2007, 2023) and two T20 World Cup titles (2021). The Australian team has dominated world cricket for decades with players like Ricky Ponting, Shane Warne, Glenn McGrath, Steve Smith, Pat Cummins, and David Warner. Australia's home grounds include the MCG, SCG, Gabba, and Perth Stadium.

England Cricket Team

England cricket has undergone a transformation since 2015, winning the 2019 Cricket World Cup and the 2022 T20 World Cup. Under coaches like Eoin Morgan and Brendon McCullum, England adopted an aggressive "Bazball" approach. Key players include Joe Root, Ben Stokes, Jofra Archer, and Harry Brook. England's home venues include Lord's, The Oval, Edgbaston, and Headingley.

Pakistan National Cricket Team

Pakistan is known for producing some of cricket's greatest talents, including Imran Khan, Wasim Akram, Waqar Younis, and Babar Azam. Pakistan won the Cricket World Cup in 1992 under Imran Khan's captaincy. The Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) oversees the sport in the country. Key venues include Gaddafi Stadium (Lahore), National Stadium (Karachi), and Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium.

South Africa Cricket Team

South Africa, known as the Proteas, has been a competitive force since their return from isolation in 1991. While they have never won a Cricket World Cup, they consistently perform well in ICC tournaments. Key players include Kagiso Rabada, Anrich Nortje, and Aiden Markram. South Africa co-hosts the 2027 Cricket World Cup.

New Zealand Cricket Team

New Zealand, known as the Black Caps, reached the 2015 and 2021 World Test Championship finals, winning the WTC in 2021. They reached the 2019 Cricket World Cup final (lost to England in a dramatic super over). Key players include Kane Williamson, Trent Boult, and Devon Conway. New Zealand's home grounds include Eden Park and Basin Reserve.

Sri Lanka Cricket Team

Sri Lanka won the 1996 Cricket World Cup under Arjuna Ranatunga's captaincy. Legendary players include Muttiah Muralitharan, Kumar Sangakkara, Mahela Jayawardene, and Sanath Jayasuriya. Sri Lanka has won the T20 World Cup once (2014) and the Asia Cup multiple times.

Bangladesh National Cricket Team

Bangladesh has improved significantly in recent years, achieving Test status in 2000 and winning major series against top teams. Key players include Shakib Al Hasan, Tamim Iqbal, and Mushfiqur Rahim. Bangladesh hosts the Bangladesh Premier League (BPL) T20 tournament.

West Indies Cricket Team

The West Indies cricket team represents a consortium of Caribbean nations. They won the Cricket World Cup twice (1975, 1979) and the T20 World Cup twice (2012, 2016). Legendary players include Viv Richards, Brian Lara, Sir Curtly Ambrose, and Chris Gayle. The West Indies are known for their entertaining brand of cricket.

Afghanistan National Cricket Team

Afghanistan is the fastest-growing cricket nation, gaining Test status in 2017. Despite limited resources, Afghanistan has produced world-class players like Rashid Khan, Mohammad Nabi, and Mujeeb Ur Rahman. They have qualified for multiple Cricket World Cups and are a competitive force in T20 cricket.

Popular Cricket Players

Virat Kohli (India)

Virat Kohli is one of the greatest batsmen in cricket history. Known as "King Kohli," he has scored over 25,000 international runs across all formats. Kohli holds the record for most ODI centuries (49) and is known for his aggressive batting style, fitness standards, and competitive spirit. He has been ICC Player of the Year multiple times and was India's captain across all formats until 2022.

Rohit Sharma (India)

Rohit Sharma, known as "Hitman," is India's current ODI and T20I captain. He holds the record for the highest individual ODI score (264) and has scored three double centuries in ODIs. Rohit is known for his elegant batting style and ability to play big innings. He has led Mumbai Indians to five IPL titles.

Steve Smith (Australia)

Steve Smith is one of the best Test batsmen of his generation. With over 9,000 Test runs and an average above 58, Smith has been ranked as the world's number one Test batsman multiple times. He was instrumental in Australia's 2019 Ashes victory and their 2023 World Cup triumph.

Joe Root (England)

Joe Root is England's most prolific modern-day Test batsman. He has scored over 11,000 Test runs and is known for his classical technique and ability to play long innings. Root was instrumental in England's 2019 World Cup victory and has been a key figure in their "Bazball" revolution.

Babar Azam (Pakistan)

Babar Azam is widely regarded as one of the best batsmen in the world across all formats. He has been ranked as the number one T20I batsman and is known for his elegant stroke play. Babar captains Pakistan in all formats and has scored over 15,000 international runs.

Kane Williamson (New Zealand)

Kane Williamson is New Zealand's greatest batsman and former captain. He led New Zealand to the 2019 World Cup final and the 2021 World Test Championship title. Known for his calm demeanor and technical excellence, Williamson has scored over 8,000 Test runs.

Pat Cummins (Australia)

Pat Cummins is Australia's current Test captain and one of the world's best fast bowlers. He led Australia to the 2023 Cricket World Cup title and has taken over 250 Test wickets. Cummins is known for his accuracy, pace, and ability to perform in pressure situations.

Ben Stokes (England)

Ben Stokes is one of cricket's greatest all-rounders. His match-winning innings in the 2019 World Cup final and his 2019 Ashes heroics at Headingley are among the greatest moments in cricket history. Stokes captains England's Test team and is known for his aggressive "Bazball" approach.

Jasprit Bumrah (India)

Jasprit Bumrah is one of the world's best fast bowlers, known for his unique bowling action and deadly yorkers. He has been instrumental in India's success in all formats and is ranked among the top bowlers in ICC rankings. Bumrah's ability to bowl at the death makes him invaluable in limited-overs cricket.

Shaheen Afridi (Pakistan)

Shaheen Shah Afridi is Pakistan's leading fast bowler, known for his pace and ability to swing the ball. He was instrumental in Pakistan's 2022 T20 World Cup final appearance and has taken over 200 international wickets. Shaheen's opening spells are feared by batsmen worldwide.

Cricket Calendar

Cricket is played year-round with different seasons and tournaments across the globe:

  • January-February: Big Bash League (Australia), SA20 (South Africa), bilateral series
  • March-May: Indian Premier League (IPL), county cricket begins (England)
  • June-August: International bilateral series, The Ashes (when in England), Caribbean Premier League
  • September-November: ICC tournaments, Asia Cup, bilateral series, start of Australian summer
  • November-December: Australian home season, Big Bash League, Test series

The ICC Future Tours Programme (FTP) schedules bilateral series and tournaments across all member nations. Check the ICC website or Sportsurge for the latest fixtures and schedules.
