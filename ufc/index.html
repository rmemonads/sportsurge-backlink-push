What Is UFC?

The Ultimate Fighting Championship, commonly known as the UFC, is the world's premier mixed martial arts (MMA) organization and one of the fastest-growing sports brands on the planet. Founded in 1993 by Art Davie, Rorion Gracie, and Bob Meyrowitz, the UFC began as a tournament to determine the most effective martial art in a real fight with minimal rules. What started as a controversial spectacle has evolved into a global sporting phenomenon that regularly fills arenas and draws millions of viewers worldwide.

The UFC was acquired by Zuffa, LLC in 2001, headed by brothers Frank and Lorenzo Fertitta, with Dana White serving as president. Under their leadership and the promotional genius of Dana White, the UFC grew from a niche attraction into a mainstream powerhouse. In 2016, talent agency WME-IMG (now Endeavor) purchased the UFC for approximately $4 billion, cementing its status as one of the most valuable sports properties in the world.

Today, the UFC hosts over 40 events per year, including numbered pay-per-view cards that feature championship bouts and marquee matchups. The organization boasts a roster of the world's most skilled fighters across multiple weight classes for both men and women. Fighters in the UFC represent diverse martial arts backgrounds including Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, wrestling, boxing, Muay Thai, karate, and judo, creating a dynamic and unpredictable style of combat that keeps fans on the edge of their seats.

UFC events are held in iconic venues around the world. The T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas serves as the UFC's home base, hosting many of the organization's biggest events including UFC 300 and numerous championship fights. Madison Square Garden in New York City has hosted several historic UFC events, while the Etihad Arena in Abu Dhabi has become the UFC's international home for events like UFC 305. Other notable venues include the Kia Forum in Los Angeles, the Scotiabank Arena in Toronto, and the O2 Arena in London.

The UFC's broadcast partnerships have been instrumental in its growth. In the United States, ESPN holds exclusive rights to UFC content through ESPN+ for pay-per-view events and ESPN networks for Fight Night cards. Internationally, the UFC is broadcast in over 165 countries and territories through partnerships with broadcasters like BT Sport in the United Kingdom, Fox Sports in Australia, and DAZN in various European markets. These partnerships ensure that fans around the world can watch UFC live stream content from anywhere. Sportsurge also covers boxing matches, NFL games, and soccer streaming for fans who follow multiple combat and mainstream sports.

The sport of MMA and the UFC have also contributed significantly to the evolution of martial arts training. Fighters now cross-train in multiple disciplines, with many athletes holding black belts in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu while also possessing elite striking skills. This evolution has created a new breed of complete martial artists who can dominate in every phase of combat, from standing exchanges to ground fighting and everything in between.

UFC Events

The UFC hosts a packed schedule of events throughout the year, ranging from weekly Fight Night cards to massive numbered pay-per-view spectacles. Understanding the different types of UFC events helps fans plan their viewing and never miss a fight.

UFC Numbered Events (Pay-Per-View)

UFC numbered events are the organization's flagship shows, featuring championship bouts, marquee matchups, and the deepest fight cards of the year. These events take place approximately 12 times per year and are broadcast as pay-per-view in the United States on ESPN+. Some of the most recent landmark events include UFC 300, which celebrated the milestone with an unforgettable card featuring Alex Pereira defending his light heavyweight title, and UFC 305 in Abu Dhabi, which brought world-class MMA to the Middle East. UFC 306, held at the Sphere in Las Vegas, pushed the boundaries of live event production with an immersive experience unlike anything seen in combat sports.

Numbered events typically feature 13-15 fights organized into a preliminary card, early prelims, and the main card. The main card consists of five fights, with the main event being a championship bout or the most anticipated matchup on the card. These events draw the largest audiences and generate the most excitement in the MMA community. Fans circle dates on their calendars for events like UFC 300, UFC 310, and future numbered cards that promise to deliver unforgettable moments.

UFC Fight Night

UFC Fight Night events are the backbone of the UFC's schedule, occurring nearly every Saturday throughout the year. These events are broadcast on ESPN and ESPN+ in the United States and feature a mix of ranked contenders, rising prospects, and veteran fighters looking to make their mark. Fight Night events often serve as a proving ground for fighters looking to earn a spot on future numbered cards.

While Fight Night events do not feature championship bouts, they frequently showcase exciting matchups with significant rankings implications. Many of the UFC's biggest stars have fought their way up through Fight Night events, and these cards often produce the Fight of the Night and Performance of the Night bonuses that can make or break a fighter's career trajectory.

UFC Contender Series

The Dana White's Contender Series (DWCS) is a talent identification program where up-and-coming fighters compete for UFC contracts. The series runs during specific seasons throughout the year and gives fighters the opportunity to impress Dana White and the UFC matchmakers with their performances. A dominant finish or impressive victory on DWCS can lead to an immediate UFC contract, making it a crucial pathway for aspiring MMA fighters.

Major UFC Venues

The UFC travels to venues around the globe for its events. The T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas is the UFC's home venue, hosting the biggest numbered events and championship fights. Madison Square Garden in New York City provides a historic backdrop for landmark events, while the Etihad Arena in Abu Dhabi has become a regular destination for international events. The Kia Forum in Los Angeles, the Scotiabank Arena in Toronto, the O2 Arena in London, and the Accor Arena in Paris are among the many world-class venues that host UFC events throughout the year.

How UFC Fights Work

Understanding the rules and structure of a UFC fight is essential for any new fan. The UFC operates under the Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts, which are recognized by athletic commissions worldwide. Here is a comprehensive breakdown of how a UFC fight works from start to finish.

Fight Duration

Championship bouts and main event fights are scheduled for five rounds, with each round lasting five minutes. All other fights are scheduled for three rounds of five minutes each. Between rounds, there is a one-minute rest period where fighters receive instructions from their corner teams. The total fight time for a championship bout is a maximum of 25 minutes of action, while regular fights can last up to 15 minutes.

Scoring System

UFC fights are scored by three judges cageside using the 10-point must system. After each round, the winner of that round receives 10 points, while the loser receives 9 or fewer. In the event of an even round, both fighters receive 10 points. Judges consider effective striking, grappling, aggression, and cage control when determining the winner of each round. At the end of the fight, the judges' scores are tallied to determine the winner by unanimous decision, split decision, or majority decision.

Ways to Win

A UFC fight can be won in several ways. A knockout (KO) occurs when a fighter is rendered unable to continue due to strikes. A technical knockout (TKO) occurs when the referee, doctor, or corner stops the fight due to a fighter's inability to intelligently defend themselves. A submission occurs when a fighter taps out or verbally submits to a joint lock or choke. A decision occurs when the fight goes the full distance and is decided by the judges' scorecards. A disqualification can occur for serious rule violations.

Legal and Illegal Techniques

Under the Unified Rules, fighters can use punches, kicks, knees, elbows, and clinch techniques. Fighters are also permitted to take opponents to the ground and apply submissions. However, several techniques are illegal, including strikes to the back of the head, spine, or groin; headbutts; eye gouges; fish-hooking; and small joint manipulation. Fighters are also prohibited from grabbing the fence or opponent's clothing. Downed fighters cannot be struck with kicks to the head, though this rule varies by jurisdiction.

The Octagon

UFC fights take place in a eight-sided enclosure known as "The Octagon." The standard Octagon measures 30 feet in diameter with a 6-foot reach surrounding the fighting area. The enclosure is surrounded by a chain-link fence and padded canvas flooring. The Octagon has become one of the most iconic symbols in all of sports, instantly recognizable to fans around the world. The unique shape and design of the Octagon were specifically created for the UFC and have remained largely unchanged since the organization's early days.

Weight Cutting and Weigh-Ins

Before every fight, fighters must weigh in to ensure they meet the weight requirements for their division. The official weigh-in takes place the day before the fight. Fighters typically undergo significant dehydration in the days leading up to the weigh-in, cutting weight to meet their division's limit. After weighing in, fighters rehydrate and recover overnight before competing the following day. Fighters who miss weight may have a percentage of their purse deducted or may have the bout cancelled depending on the severity of the miss and the athletic commission's rules.
