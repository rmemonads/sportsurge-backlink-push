What Is WWE?

World Wrestling Entertainment, universally known as WWE, is the largest and most recognized professional wrestling promotion in the world. Founded by Vince McMahon Sr. in 1953 as the Capitol Wrestling Corporation, WWE has grown from a regional territory into a global entertainment powerhouse that generates over a billion dollars in annual revenue and reaches audiences in more than 180 countries across the globe.

WWE blends athletic competition, theatrical storytelling, and larger-than-life characters to create a unique form of entertainment that captivates millions of fans every week. The product features scripted storylines that unfold across weekly television shows and premium live events, with wrestlers portraying heroes known as babyfaces and villains known as heels. This combination of sport and entertainment has made WWE one of the most recognizable brands in all of pop culture.

Headquartered in Stamford, Connecticut, WWE produces over 500 hours of original content each year across its three flagship shows: Monday Night RAW on the USA Network, Friday Night SmackDown on FOX, and NXT on the USA Network. The company also produces monthly premium live events, formerly known as pay-per-views, that are available on the Peacock streaming platform in the United States and on the WWE Network internationally.

WWE has been home to some of the most iconic figures in entertainment history, including Hulk Hogan, The Rock, Stone Cold Steve Austin, John Cena, The Undertaker, and Triple H, who now serves as the company's Chief Content Officer. These larger-than-life characters have transcended professional wrestling to become mainstream celebrities, appearing in Hollywood blockbusters, video games, and pop culture references that endure for decades.

The company's flagship event, WrestleMania, is one of the biggest annual spectacles in sports and entertainment, regularly selling out stadiums with over 60,000 fans and generating hundreds of millions of dollars in revenue. WWE events are held at iconic venues across the world, including Madison Square Garden in New York, the O2 Arena in London, the Tokyo Dome in Japan, and stadium shows in Saudi Arabia, Australia, and Canada. Whether you are a lifelong fan or just discovering professional wrestling, WWE offers an unparalleled entertainment experience that combines athleticism, drama, and storytelling in ways no other form of entertainment can match. WWE Shows

WWE produces several weekly television shows and premium live events throughout the year. Each show has its own unique identity, roster, and storylines, creating a rich and interconnected wrestling universe that fans can follow across multiple platforms.

Monday Night RAW

Monday Night RAW is WWE's flagship program and the longest-running weekly episodic television show in history. Since its debut on January 11, 1993, RAW has been the cornerstone of WWE programming, airing live every Monday night. Currently broadcast on the USA Network, RAW features three hours of live wrestling action, compelling storylines, and appearances from the biggest stars in the industry. RAW is home to many of WWE's top champions and titleholders, and the show regularly features dramatic title defenses, surprise returns, and shocking moments that set the wrestling world buzzing. The show typically originates from major arenas across the United States and Canada, with crowds that bring electric energy to every episode.

Friday Night SmackDown

Friday Night SmackDown is WWE's second flagship show, airing live every Friday night on FOX. Originally debuting in 1999, SmackDown has evolved into a must-watch program that often rivals or surpasses RAW in ratings and critical acclaim. SmackDown is known for its fast-paced action, tight storytelling, and strong in-ring competition. The show features its own distinct roster of superstars and championship titles, creating a unique identity that sets it apart from RAW. SmackDown has been home to some of the most memorable moments in WWE history, including the rise of Roman Reigns as the Tribal Chief, the debut of The Fiend, and the historic Blue Brand invasions. With a two-hour format, SmackDown delivers concise, impactful television that keeps fans engaged from start to finish.

NXT

NXT is WWE's developmental brand and third weekly television show, airing on Tuesday nights on the USA Network. Originally launched in 2012 as a reality competition show, NXT has evolved into a critically acclaimed wrestling program that many fans consider the best in-ring product in all of professional wrestling. NXT serves as the proving ground for the next generation of WWE superstars, featuring a mix of established veterans and rising prospects who are honing their craft before making the jump to RAW or SmackDown. The brand has produced numerous future world champions, including Seth Rollins, Roman Reigns, Becky Lynch, and Bayley. NXT's unique identity is defined by its emphasis on technical wrestling, innovative match types, and a passionate fanbase known as the NXT Universe that fills arenas with deafening chants and unforgettable energy. Major NXT events like NXT TakeOver have become some of the most critically acclaimed shows in wrestling history.

NXT TakeOver and NXT Special Events

NXT has its own schedule of premium live events that showcase the brand's best talent in matches that frequently receive five-star ratings from wrestling critics. NXT TakeOver events have been held at iconic venues during WrestleMania and Survivor Series weekends, as well as standalone specials throughout the year. These events have featured some of the greatest matches in WWE history, including the legendary bouts between Johnny Gargano and Tommaso Ciampa, the war games matches, and the breakout performances of future main roster superstars. NXT specials continue to deliver some of the most innovative and hard-hitting professional wrestling anywhere in the world.

WWE Main Event and Superstars

WWE also produces several secondary programs that give additional superstars television time and allow fans to see more of their favorite wrestlers. WWE Main Event features matches between RAW and SmackDown superstars, while WWE Superstars showcases talent from across all brands. These shows provide important developmental opportunities for rising stars and allow established performers to develop new characters and storylines in a lower-pressure environment.

WWE Championships

Championship titles are the lifeblood of professional wrestling, representing the pinnacle of achievement in WWE. Each title carries its own prestigious history and is defended across different brands and events. Here are the major championships in WWE:

World Heavyweight Championship

The World Heavyweight Championship is one of the most prestigious titles in WWE history. Originally introduced in 2002 and reintroduced in 2023, this championship represents the top prize on Monday Night RAW. The title has been held by legendary performers including Triple H, Batista, Edge, and most recently Gunther, who brought a hard-hitting European style to the championship. The World Heavyweight Championship is defended on RAW and at premium live events, with Gunther establishing himself as a dominant champion through his incredible technical wrestling ability and imposing presence.

WWE Championship

The WWE Championship is the most iconic title in professional wrestling history, dating back to 1963 when Buddy Rogers became the first champion. This title has been held by every major star in WWE history, from Hulk Hogan to Stone Cold Steve Austin, from The Rock to John Cena. The WWE Championship is currently part of the SmackDown brand and is the top prize on the blue brand. Cody Rhodes, who completed his story by winning the title at WrestleMania 40, currently holds this prestigious championship, having dethroned Roman Reigns in one of the most emotionally charged main events in WrestleMania history.

WWE Women's World Championship

The Women's World Championship represents the pinnacle of women's wrestling on Monday Night RAW. This title has been held by some of the most talented female performers in WWE history, including Becky Lynch, Bianca Belair, and Rhea Ripley. The championship has helped elevate women's wrestling to new heights, with main event caliber matches that rival anything on the men's side. Rhea Ripley, with her dominant run as champion, has helped establish the Women's World Championship as one of the most prestigious titles in all of WWE.

WWE Women's Championship

The WWE Women's Championship is the top title for female performers on Friday Night SmackDown. This championship has been held by iconic women including Charlotte Flair, Asuka, and Bayley. The Women's Championship represents the SmackDown women's division and is defended at premium live events with matches that consistently steal the show. The title has been central to some of the most compelling storylines in recent WWE history, with Bayley's historic run as champion being a highlight of the SmackDown brand.

WWE Intercontinental Championship

The Intercontinental Championship is one of the most storied secondary titles in WWE history. Known as the workhorse title, it has been held by some of the greatest in-ring performers of all time, including Randy Savage, Shawn Michaels, Bret Hart, and Chris Jericho. Gunther held the Intercontinental Championship for over 600 days before transitioning to the World Heavyweight Championship, setting a modern record that cemented the title's prestige. The Intercontinental Championship is currently defended on SmackDown and represents technical excellence and championship caliber wrestling.

WWE United States Championship

The United States Championship is the secondary title on Monday Night RAW, representing American wrestling tradition. This title has been held by legendary performers including Ric Flair, Ricky Steamboat, and more recently, stars like LA Knight, who brought the title to new prominence with his charismatic promos and hard-hitting matches. The United States Championship provides a stepping stone for rising stars looking to prove themselves and has been defended in some of the most exciting matches on RAW programming.

WWE Tag Team Championships

The WWE Tag Team Championships represent the pinnacle of tag team wrestling across both RAW and SmackDown. Tag team wrestling has been a cornerstone of WWE since the days of the Fabulous Freebirds and the Hart Foundation. Current tag team champions continue this tradition, delivering exciting two-on-two action that showcases teamwork, chemistry, and innovative double-team maneuvers. The tag team division has experienced a renaissance in recent years, with teams like The Judgment Day, The Bloodline, and DIY providing compelling competition for the gold.

NXT Championship and NXT Women's Championship

The NXT Championship and NXT Women's Championship represent the top prizes in WWE's developmental brand. These titles have been held by future main event stars including Seth Rollins, Kevin Owens, Sami Zayn, Bianca Belair, and Rhea Ripley before their ascension to RAW and SmackDown. NXT championships carry significant prestige and are defended at TakeOver events with matches that frequently receive critical acclaim. Winning an NXT championship is often the first step in a superstar's journey to becoming a main roster world champion.

WWE Events Calendar

WWE produces a packed schedule of premium live events throughout the year, each offering a unique experience and high-stakes matches. Here is the typical annual WWE calendar:

January – Royal Rumble

The Royal Rumble kicks off the new year in January with one of WWE's most popular and anticipated events. The main attraction is the 30-person Royal Rumble match, where superstars enter at timed intervals and are eliminated by being thrown over the top rope. The winners of the men's and women's Royal Rumble matches earn championship opportunities at WrestleMania. The event also features championship defenses and grudge matches that set the stage for the Road to WrestleMania. The Royal Rumble is consistently one of the highest-rated WWE events of the year, with surprise entrants and dramatic eliminations creating moments that fans talk about for months.

February/March – Elimination Chamber

The Elimination Chamber is one of the most dangerous and physically demanding matches in WWE. Six superstars are locked inside a massive steel structure with chain-link pods and fight for survival. The last superstar remaining wins the match and often earns a championship opportunity at WrestleMania. The Elimination Chamber match has produced some of the most brutal and memorable moments in WWE history, with superstars enduring punishment from the unforgiving structure. The event typically takes place in February or March as part of the Road to WrestleMania.

March/April – WrestleMania

WrestleMania is the granddaddy of them all, the biggest event in professional wrestling and one of the largest annual sporting events in the world. Held annually in March or April, WrestleMania takes place in massive stadiums and arenas across the United States and occasionally in international locations. The two-night event features the biggest matches, most dramatic storylines, and highest production values in all of professional wrestling. WrestleMania has been the site of some of the most iconic moments in wrestling history, including Hulk Hogan body slamming Andre the Giant, Stone Cold Steve Austin vs. The Rock, and Roman Reigns' historic championship reign. The event regularly draws over 60,000 fans per night and generates hundreds of millions of dollars in revenue.

April/May – Backlash and King and Queen of the Ring

Following WrestleMania, WWE produces several events that continue the momentum from the biggest show of the year. Backlash typically features rematches and new feuds that emerge from WrestleMania's aftermath. King and Queen of the Ring is a tournament-based event where superstars compete in single elimination matches to earn the prestigious crown and a championship opportunity at SummerSlam. These events help establish the direction for the summer months and often feature surprising results that reshape the landscape of WWE programming.

June/July – Money in the Bank

Money in the Bank is one of the most exciting and unpredictable events in WWE. The signature Money in the Bank ladder match features superstars climbing ladders to retrieve a briefcase containing a contract for a world championship match at any time and place of their choosing. The guaranteed title opportunity creates constant suspense, as the briefcase holder can cash in at any moment, even during another superstar's championship defense. Money in the Bank has produced some of the most dramatic cash-ins in wrestling history, including Edge's first-ever cash-in at New Year's Revolution 2005. The event also features championship defenses and grudge matches that set the stage for SummerSlam.

August – SummerSlam

SummerSlam, known as The Biggest Party of the Summer, is one of WWE's Big Five events alongside WrestleMania, Royal Rumble, Survivor Series, and Money in the Bank. Held annually in August, SummerSlam typically features major championship matches, dream bouts, and the culmination of summer-long feuds. The event has been the site of some of the most memorable moments in WWE history, including Brock Lesnar ending The Undertaker's WrestleMania streak in a different context, and numerous title changes that reshaped the company. SummerSlam regularly draws massive audiences and is one of the highest-grossing events on the WWE calendar.

September/October – Payback and Fastlane

Payback and Fastlane are events that typically take place in the fall, featuring rematches from SummerSlam and setting the stage for Survivor Series. These events often feature championship defenses and grudge matches that advance ongoing storylines. While not considered Big Five events, Payback and Fastlane have produced memorable moments and important title changes that have shaped the direction of WWE programming heading into the final months of the year.

November – Survivor Series

Survivor Series is one of WWE's Big Five events, traditionally featuring elimination-style tag team matches where superstars from RAW and SmackDown compete for brand supremacy. While the traditional elimination format has evolved over the years, Survivor Series continues to be a major event that often features champion vs. champion matches and cross-brand rivalries. The event has been the site of some of the most memorable moments in WWE history, including The Undertaker's debut and the invasion of WCW and ECW superstars. In recent years, Survivor Series has also incorporated WarGames matches, adding an extra layer of intensity and excitement to the event.

December – TLC and Year-End Events

WWE closes out the year with events like TLC: Tables, Ladders, and Chairs, which features matches that utilize those specific weapons. The holiday season also brings special episodes of RAW and SmackDown, as well as year-end awards and retrospectives. December events often set the stage for the Royal Rumble and the beginning of the Road to WrestleMania, making them important for establishing the direction of WWE programming heading into the new year.

AEW – WWE's Rival Promotion

All Elite Wrestling, commonly known as AEW, is the largest professional wrestling promotion to compete with WWE in over two decades. Founded in 2019 by Tony Khan, AEW has quickly established itself as a major force in the wrestling industry, offering an alternative product that emphasizes athletic competition, organic storytelling, and a more wrestling-focused approach. AEW airs its flagship shows, AEW Dynamite on TNT and AEW Collision on TNT, providing fans with additional wrestling programming throughout the week.

AEW features many former WWE superstars who have found success in the new promotion, including Chris Jericho, CM Punk before his return to WWE, Jon Moxley who was known as Dean Ambrose in WWE, and the Young Bucks who were previously in WWE's developmental system. The promotion also features homegrown talent like Kenny Omega, Hangman Adam Page, MJF, and Swerve Strickland who have become major stars in their own right. AEW's roster depth and commitment to in-ring excellence have produced some of the highest-rated matches and moments in modern wrestling history.

While AEW and WWE are competitors, the existence of both promotions has been beneficial for wrestling fans as a whole. The competition has driven both companies to produce better content, offer more opportunities for wrestlers, and innovate in ways that push the industry forward. Many fans enjoy watching both promotions, appreciating the different styles and approaches each brings to professional wrestling. Whether you prefer WWE's entertainment-focused presentation or AEW's wrestling-centric approach, the current era offers an unprecedented wealth of professional wrestling content for fans to enjoy.
