What Is the NFL?

The National Football League, commonly known as the NFL, is the premier professional American football league in the United States and one of the most popular sports leagues in the world. Founded in 1920 as the American Professional Football Association before renaming to the NFL in 1922, the league has grown into a massive entertainment juggernaut that captivates millions of fans every season.

The NFL consists of 32 teams divided equally between the American Football Conference (AFC) and the National Football Conference (NFC). Each conference is further divided into four divisions: North, South, East, and West, with four teams in each division. This structure ensures a balanced and competitive schedule throughout the 18-week regular season.

NFL games are played on Sundays, Mondays, and Thursdays during the regular season. Sunday games kick off at 1:00 PM and 4:05 PM ET, with the featured Sunday Night Football matchup starting at 8:20 PM ET. Monday Night Football on ESPN and Thursday Night Football on Amazon Prime Video provide midweek action that keeps fans engaged throughout the week.

The NFL season culminates in the Super Bowl, one of the most-watched annual sporting events globally, attracting over 100 million viewers in the United States alone. The league generates billions of dollars in revenue through television contracts, sponsorships, merchandise sales, and ticket revenue, making it the wealthiest professional sports league in the world.

From the iconic Green Bay Packers at Lambeau Field to the Las Vegas Raiders at Allegiant Stadium, from the Dallas Cowboys at AT&T Stadium to the Buffalo Bills at Highmark Stadium, the NFL offers some of the most electric atmospheres in all of sports. Whether you are watching from the stands or streaming from home, the NFL delivers unmatched excitement every week.

All 32 NFL Teams

The NFL features 32 teams across two conferences. Here is the complete list of every NFL team organized by conference and division:

American Football Conference (AFC)

The AFC consists of 16 teams. The AFC East includes the Buffalo Bills, Miami Dolphins, New England Patriots, and New York Jets. The AFC North features the Baltimore Ravens, Cincinnati Bengals, Cleveland Browns, and Pittsburgh Steelers. The AFC South comprises the Houston Texans, Indianapolis Colts, Jacksonville Jaguars, and Tennessee Titans. The AFC West includes the Denver Broncos, Kansas City Chiefs, Las Vegas Raiders, and Los Angeles Chargers.

National Football Conference (NFC)

The NFC also has 16 teams. The NFC East features the Dallas Cowboys, New York Giants, Philadelphia Eagles, and Washington Commanders. The NFC North includes the Chicago Bears, Detroit Lions, Green Bay Packers, and Minnesota Vikings. The NFC South comprises the Atlanta Falcons, Carolina Panthers, New Orleans Saints, and Tampa Bay Buccaneers. The NFC West includes the Arizona Cardinals, Los Angeles Rams, San Francisco 49ers, and Seattle Seahawks.

Each team plays 17 regular season games and has one bye week. The schedule includes divisional matchups, conference games, and inter-conference contests, ensuring that every team faces a variety of opponents throughout the season.

NFL Season Format

The NFL season is divided into several distinct phases, each with its own excitement and importance. Understanding the format helps fans appreciate the journey from the regular season to the Super Bowl.

Preseason (August)

The NFL preseason features four weeks of exhibition games that give teams a chance to evaluate their rosters and prepare for the regular season. Preseason games are played in August and feature a mix of starters, backups, and players fighting for roster spots. While the preseason has been reduced from four to three games in recent years, it remains an important part of the NFL calendar.

Regular Season (September – January)

The 18-week regular season is the heart of the NFL. Each of the 32 teams plays 17 games with one bye week. The schedule is carefully constructed to ensure competitive balance, with teams playing divisional rivals twice per season and rotating opponents from other divisions and conferences. The regular season determines playoff seeding and includes some of the most anticipated matchups of the year.

Playoffs (January – February)

Seven teams from each conference qualify for the playoffs: four division winners and three wild card teams. The playoffs consist of three rounds – Wild Card Weekend, Divisional Round, and Conference Championships – leading up to the Super Bowl. The playoff format creates intense, win-or-go-home scenarios that produce some of the most memorable moments in NFL history.

Super Bowl (February)

The Super Bowl is the championship game of the NFL, pitting the AFC Champion against the NFC Champion. Held annually on the first Sunday in February, it is the most-watched television event in the United States. The Super Bowl features not only the game itself but also elaborate halftime performances, celebrity appearances, and iconic commercials that have become a cultural phenomenon.

Offseason (February – August)

The NFL offseason includes the NFL Draft, free agency, and offseason workout programs. The NFL Draft, held in late April, is a three-day event where teams select eligible college players. Free agency begins in mid-March and allows teams to sign unrestricted free agents. The offseason is a busy time for NFL front offices as they build their rosters for the upcoming season.

NFL Playoffs

The NFL Playoffs are one of the most exciting postseason tournaments in professional sports. Since the league expanded to 14 teams in 2020, the playoffs have become even more competitive and dramatic. The format ensures that only the best teams advance, creating must-watch matchups every weekend.

Wild Card Weekend

Wild Card Weekend is the opening round of the NFL Playoffs. Six games are played featuring the second and third seeds from each conference hosting the wild card teams. The top seed in each conference receives a bye and does not play until the Divisional Round. Wild Card Weekend games are known for their intensity and often produce upsets that shake up the playoff bracket.

Divisional Round

The Divisional Round features four games with the top seeds joining the remaining teams. The number one seeds host the lowest remaining wild card teams, while the number two seeds face the other remaining teams. Divisional Round games typically feature the best matchups of the playoffs, with Super Bowl contenders separating themselves from pretenders.

Conference Championships

The AFC and NFC Championship Games determine which teams will advance to the Super Bowl. These games are among the most-watched sporting events of the year, with the winners earning the right to compete for the Lombardi Trophy. The atmosphere at conference championship games is electric, with fans creating deafening noise and unforgettable experiences.

Pro Bowl

The Pro Bowl is the NFL's all-star game, featuring the best players from the AFC and NFC. While the format has changed over the years, the Pro Bowl remains a celebration of NFL talent and a chance for fans to see their favorite players in a less competitive setting. The Pro Bowl Skills Showcase has become a popular event, featuring competitions that highlight the athleticism and personality of NFL players.

The Super Bowl

The Super Bowl is the annual championship game of the National Football League and one of the most significant sporting events in the world. First played on January 15, 1967, following the AFL-NFL merger, the Super Bowl has grown from a modest football game into a global cultural phenomenon that transcends sports.

The game features the champions of the AFC and NFC competing for the Vince Lombardi Trophy, named after the legendary Green Bay Packers coach. The Super Bowl is typically played on the first Sunday in February at a pre-selected neutral site. Iconic venues that have hosted the Super Bowl include AT&T Stadium in Arlington, home of the Dallas Cowboys; SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, home of the Los Angeles Rams and Los Angeles Chargers; and Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas, home of the Las Vegas Raiders.

The Super Bowl halftime show has become one of the most anticipated musical performances of the year. Past performers include Beyonce, Bruno Mars, The Weeknd, Rihanna, and Usher. The commercials shown during the Super Bowl are also major cultural events, with companies paying millions of dollars for 30-second spots and creating elaborate, memorable advertisements.

Some of the most memorable Super Bowl moments include the New England Patriots' 28-3 comeback against the Atlanta Falcons in Super Bowl LI, the Philadelphia Eagles' first Super Bowl victory in Super Bowl LII, and the Kansas City Chiefs' dramatic overtime win against the San Francisco 49ers in Super Bowl LVIII. These moments have cemented the Super Bowl as one of the greatest spectacles in sports.
